## 中文

> ### 流動是江河唯一的出路，海是江河必然的歸宿；  
> ### 流動是無常直接的展現，空是無常如實的本性；  
> ### 流動是世間永恆的真實，滅是世間終將的去向；  
> ### 流動是開悟最美的姿勢，禪是開悟不二的法門。  

> ### 變是唯一的不變。


1. 「流動」與「無常」（Anicca）
禪修的核心觀照之一就是無常。文中提到的「流動」正是無常最生動的展現。在禪修中，我們觀察呼吸、念頭、感受，發現它們沒有一刻是靜止的。承認「變是唯一的不變」，就是放下對「永恆實體」的執著。當你不再試圖截斷流水，而是隨緣流動，痛苦就失去了依附點。
2. 「海」與「空性」（Sunyata）
「海是江河必然的歸宿」與「空是無常如實的本性」對應。在禪宗裡，江河象徵「現象」（色），大海象徵「本體」（空）。河流入海，自我也就融入了大我（或無我）。禪修的目的，就是讓人體悟到個體生命（河）本就與宇宙真理（海）是一體的，這種回歸感就是涅槃的意象。
3. 「滅」與「寂靜」（Nirodha）
「滅是世間終將的去向」並非指消亡，而是指火燄熄滅後的平靜。在禪修中，這代表煩惱的止息。當我們看透了流動的本質，不再與現實對抗，紛擾的心念自然「消滅」，轉化為一種清明的定力。
4. 「姿勢」與「法門」
「流動是開悟最美的姿勢」這句話極具張力。它暗示開悟不是一種僵硬的坐姿或死水的平靜，而是一種「動態的平衡」。
禪：是不二法門，即是教我們如何不落兩邊（不執著於有，也不執著於無）。
行禪/坐禪：都是為了練習在流動的生命中，保持那一顆不被境轉的「初心」。
總結
這段話描述的是一種「無所住而生其心」的境界。
它告訴我們：
修行不是為了停止生命河流的動盪，而是學會像水一樣流動。
真正的定力，不是拒絕變化，而是在變化的洪流中，依然知道自己正奔向大海。
這確實是高級別的禪修心態：順應無常，體證空性，在流動中見證永恆。


1. “Flow” and Impermanence (Anicca)

One of the core contemplations in Zen meditation is impermanence.
The “flow” described in the text is the most vivid manifestation of anicca.

In meditation we observe the breath, thoughts, and sensations, and realize that none of them remain still for even a moment. To acknowledge that “change is the only unchanging” is to release our attachment to the idea of a permanent entity.

When we stop trying to block the river and instead learn to move with it, suffering loses the ground on which it depends.

2. “The Sea” and Emptiness (Śūnyatā)

The line “the sea is the inevitable home of the river” corresponds to “emptiness is the true nature of impermanence.”

In Zen, the river symbolizes phenomena (form), while the sea symbolizes the ultimate nature (emptiness). When the river enters the sea, the small self dissolves into the greater reality—into non-self.

The purpose of practice is to realize that individual life, like a river, has always been one with the universal truth, like the ocean.
This sense of return is an image of nirvāṇa.

3. “Cessation” and Tranquility (Nirodha)

“Cessation is the final direction of the world” does not mean annihilation, but the peace after a flame has gone out.

In meditation this refers to the ending of afflictions.
When we see through the nature of flow and stop resisting reality, the restless mind naturally “ceases,” transforming into a clear and luminous stillness.

4. “Posture” and the Dharma Gate

The phrase “flow is the most beautiful posture of awakening” carries great depth.
It suggests that awakening is not a rigid sitting position nor a stagnant calm, but a dynamic balance.

Zen is the non-dual gate: it teaches us not to fall to either side—neither clinging to existence nor to emptiness.

Walking meditation and sitting meditation are both trainings to remain rooted in the original mind while life continues to move.

Conclusion

This passage describes the state of “abiding nowhere, yet giving rise to the mind.”

It tells us:

Practice is not to stop the turbulence of the river of life, but to learn to flow like water.

True stability is not the rejection of change, but the awareness—within the current of change—that we are already moving toward the sea.

This is indeed a mature Zen attitude:
to accord with impermanence, realize emptiness, and witness the eternal within the flowing moment.

不是我在流動，  
而是「我」本身就是流動的一個暫時名字。

修行並非把心固定在某處，  
而是看見一切本就不曾停留。  
當念頭被允許像江水一樣經過，  
「我」便只是岸邊的一個概念。

## English

Flow is the only path of the river; the sea is its inevitable home.  
Flow is the direct face of impermanence; emptiness is its true nature.  
Flow is the eternal truth of the world; cessation is its final direction.  
Flow is the most graceful posture of awakening; Zen is the non-dual gate.

**Change is the only unchanging.**

> It is not that *I* am flowing,  
> but that “I” is only a temporary name of flow.

Practice is not to hold the mind in place,  
but to see that nothing has ever stayed.  
When thoughts are allowed to pass like a river,  
the “self” becomes only a concept on the shore.
