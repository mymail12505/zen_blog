# **Breath, Mind, and Non-Attachment**  
# **守息与无所依**

## **English**

In meditation practice, the breath is more than a physical rhythm —  
it is a doorway into stillness. Yet the way we relate to the breath  
determines whether the mind scatters, tightens, or becomes truly calm.

The great Tiantai master Zhizhe taught:

**“If you hold onto the movement of wind, the mind scatters.  
If you cling to the coarse breath, the mind becomes knotted.  
If you fixate on the subtle qi, the mind becomes strained.  
But if you rest gently with the natural breath, the mind becomes settled.  
To calm the mind is to rely on nothing.”**

To “hold wind” is to chase sensations.  
To “hold coarse breath” is to over-control.  
To “hold qi” is to strain for results.

But to **rest with the natural breath** is to return to balance —  
neither suppressing nor forcing.  
Like watching a cloud drift across the sky,  
the breath is simply observed, untouched.

Ultimately, Zhizhe points to the heart of meditation:  
**true calm arises only when the mind has no place to cling.**  
Breath is only the raft, not the shore.  
When attachment falls away, clarity appears by itself.

## **中文**

在禅修中，呼吸不仅是身体的节律，  
更是一扇进入寂静的门。  
然而我们如何“对待呼吸”，  
决定心是散乱、紧结，还是安然清净。

天台智者大师开示：

**「守风则散，守喘则结，守气则劳，守息则定。调心以无所依。」**

“守风”——执著于风动、身感，心随境转而散乱；  
“守喘”——着力控制粗重之气，使心情绪紧绷；  
“守气”——追逐细微之气，反令身心疲劳；  
唯有“守息”——自然、轻安地随息出入，  
身心才真正回到安稳、平衡。

智者大师最后一句点破要旨：  
**调心之道，在于“无所依”。**  
呼吸只是方便，不是终点；  
当不再执著于任何对象时，  
真正的定与明，便自然显现。

---

## **Closing Reflection · 余思**

Breath is the guide,  
but non-attachment is the true teacher.

呼吸引路，  
但“无所依”才是真正的导师。
