>### 居一切时不起妄念，于诸妄心亦不息灭；住妄想境，不加了知；于无了知，不辨真实 
** 不与妄念交战***
——从《圆觉经》看禅修的关键
“居一切时不起妄念，于诸妄心亦不息灭；住妄想境，不加了知；于无了知，不辨真实。”

《圆觉经》中有一句极易被误解，却极其深刻的话：


乍看之下，仿佛是在说：
既不起妄念，也不灭妄念；
住在妄想中，却不去“知道”；
甚至连“无知”中，也不分辨真实与否。

这听起来近乎矛盾，但它正指出了禅修中最容易走偏的一点。
---
### 像跟影子打架一样

修行人最常见的状态，是跟自己的念头打架。

起了妄念 → 想压下去

念头再起 → 更用力对抗

越对抗 → 心越乱、越疲惫

这就像跟影子打架一样。
影子不是敌人，但你越挥拳，它越跟着你动。

你能打赢影子吗？
你能靠“用力”让影子消失吗？

不能。
---
### 妄念不是问题，被卷进去才是

《圆觉经》并没有教你消灭妄念，而是指出：

妄念会起，这是自然

关键不是“起不起”

而是你有没有被它卷进漩涡

当你开始评价、分析、纠正、排斥一个念头时，
你已经站在漩涡中央了。
---

### 识着五蕴，而不流入其中

佛法中说的“五蕴”——
色、受、想、行、识，
正是这个漩涡的结构。

身体的感觉在动（色、受）

画面和记忆出现（想）

内在推动与反应升起（行）

“我在想”“我在烦”的认同感出现（识）

《圆觉经》并不是要你“切断五蕴”，
而是要你识着五蕴，却不住五蕴。

看到它们在运作，
却不跳进剧情里演主角。
---
### 空花雪月：不是没有，而是不实

经中常用比喻：空花、雪月、水中月。

空中花，看似存在

水中月，清晰却不可得

妄念也是如此。
它出现，但并不需要你去“处理”。

你一旦想处理，
就已经把“空花”当成了实物。
---
### 禅修的真正姿态

所以，禅修不是：

❌ 清空大脑

❌ 消灭念头

❌ 强行专注

而是：

✔ 念起，不随

✔ 念灭，不追

✔ 不加了知

✔ 不辨真实

就像坐在河岸边，
看水流过，
而不是跳下去逆流而上。
---

### Not Fighting the Mind
A Zen Reflection on the Sutra of Perfect Enlightenment

The Sutra of Perfect Enlightenment says:

“At all times, do not give rise to deluded thoughts;
yet toward deluded minds, do not seek their extinction.
Abide in the realm of delusion without adding knowing;
within non-knowing, do not distinguish what is real.”

At first glance, this sounds paradoxical.
How can one neither give rise to delusion nor eliminate it?

Yet this passage points directly to the core mistake in meditation practice.
---
### Like Fighting Your Own Shadow

Many meditators spend their practice fighting thoughts.

A thought arises → suppress it

It comes back → try harder

The harder you fight → the more exhausted you become

This is like fighting your own shadow.
The shadow is not your enemy.
The more you swing your arms, the more it moves.

Can you ever win?
---

### Thoughts Are Not the Problem

Being Pulled In Is

The sutra does not teach the destruction of thoughts.
It reveals something subtler:

Thoughts will arise naturally

The problem is not their appearance

The problem is being drawn into their whirlpool

The moment you judge, analyze, or try to fix a thought,
you are already inside it.
---
### Recognizing the Five Aggregates Without Entering Them

The Buddhist teaching of the Five Aggregates—
form, feeling, perception, volition, consciousness—
describes this whirlpool precisely.

Meditation is not about stopping these processes,
but about recognizing them without identification.

They function.
You see them.
But you do not become them.
---

### Sky Flowers and Reflected Moons

Zen often uses metaphors like flowers in the sky or the moon reflected in water.

They appear.
They seem real.
But they cannot be grasped.

Thoughts are the same.
They do not need correction.
They dissolve when not taken as real.
---
### The True Posture of Meditation

Meditation is not:

❌ Emptying the mind

❌ Destroying thoughts

❌ Forcing concentration

It is:

✔ Thoughts arise, you do not follow

✔ Thoughts fade, you do not chase

✔ No added knowing

✔ No need to define reality

Like sitting on a riverbank,
watching the current flow—
not jumping in to fight the stream.
